There are thousands of photos of Table Mountain, but the most famous one, the mountain stretched out across the bay with the sea in front, is taken from right here: the beaches of Table View and Bloubergstrand. From the city you stand at the foot of the mountain. From Blouberg you see it whole, flanked by Devil's Peak on the left and Lion's Head on the right, with Robben Island out on the water.

## When the light is best

Late afternoon and sunset are the magic hours. As the sun drops into the Atlantic behind you, the mountain catches the last light and turns from grey-blue to soft pink and violet. That colour change is where Blouberg ("blue mountain" in Afrikaans) gets its name. In the Cape summer the sun sets around 8 pm, in winter around 6 pm, so plan to be on the beach about an hour before.

On windy summer days, the south-easter often pours a layer of cloud over the summit, the famous "tablecloth". It is worth a photo in itself, and it usually means the kitesurfers are out in front of you too.

## Where to stand

Our favourite spots are along the beachfront between Table View and Bloubergstrand. Walk down to the waterline at low tide and you get reflections of the mountain in the wet sand. A little further north, around Big Bay, you can frame the mountain with kites and surfers. A wide-angle lens is nice, but a phone does the job beautifully.

## Our tip

Take a blanket, something to drink and the family, and stay until the lights of the city come on across the bay. From Be My Guest Lodge the beach is only a few minutes away, so you can be back at the braai by the pool before dark. Ask us at breakfast where the light should be best that day.
